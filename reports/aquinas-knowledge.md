### Aquinas: immateriality explains knowing and its degree

Source reading: [Aquinas, ST I q.14 a.1: immateriality explains cognition and its degrees](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/blob/9eef0b04b958c1c5a618b8307ef43343495591bf/corpus/claims/aquinas-st-i-q14-a1-immateriality-reason-of-knowledge.json)  
Recorded source-reading status: formalized  
Locator: I q.14 a.1 co.: immaterialitas alicuius rei est ratio quod sit cognoscitiva

The article relates cognitive ability and its degree to immateriality: plants are described as lacking cognition because of materiality; sense receives forms without matter; intellect is further removed from matter; God, already assumed highest in immateriality, is said to have the highest cognition. The passage does not define 'altogether immaterial' as both not matter–form composite and not a component of anything, or assert for every arbitrary bearer that those two negations guarantee intellect. Those are analyst criteria/bridge steps in STMT-IMMATERIAL-IS-INTELLECTUAL, now linked only by non-deductive support.

Recorded source-reading status: formalized  
Locator: I q.14 a.1 co.: cognoscens natum est habere formam etiam rei alterius

The capacity wording natum est is represented by a possible form and reception mode, not an actual reception event. Another thing is not required to differ in species from the recipient. This implication does not define cognition, assert omniscience, or equate cognition and intellect.
