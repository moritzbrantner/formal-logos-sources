### Seeking or resting in the intellectually apprehended good belongs to will

Source reading: [I q.19 a.1 co. and ad 2 — Seeking or resting in the intellectually apprehended good belongs to will](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/blob/190a6bd0c7a9db35851c73a0df8d4c09adb882ed/corpus/claims/1abe51c0-a149-402b-9e48-313ee158a2fe.json)  
Recorded source-reading status: formalized  
Locator: I q.19 a.1 co. and ad 2

The analogy with a thing’s orientation to its natural perfection supports will in an intellectual nature and its application to God. In God the text ascribes love and delight in the possessed good, with no deficiency implied. I q.80 a.2 describes passive appetite in the soul. I q.82 a.1 ad 2 calls will intellectual insofar as it wills something naturally. That reply starts with will already in view.

### Whatever HAS\_INTELLECT\_1 HAS\_WILL\_1

Source reading: [Thomas Aquinas, Summa theologiae I q.19 a.1…: Whatever HAS\_INTELLECT\_1 HAS\_WILL\_1](https://github.com/moritzbrantner/formal-logos/blob/190a6bd0c7a9db35851c73a0df8d4c09adb882ed/corpus/claims/aquinas-st-i-q19-a1-will-follows-intellect.json)  
Recorded source-reading status: formalized  
Locator: I q.19 a.1 co.: in quolibet habente intellectum, est voluntas

Will follows intellect: as a natural thing tends to its form when it lacks it and rests in it when it has it, so an intellectual nature tends to and rests in the good apprehended through an intelligible form, and both belong to will. So in whatever has intellect there is will, and since God has intellect, he has will.
